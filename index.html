Dear Teachers and Professors,

Happy Teacher's Day 🌸

There are lessons which the schoolroom teaches, and lessons which the lecture hall and the laboratory teach in their turn; and I have been fortunate, beyond the common allotment of fortune, to have received instruction in all three chambers; for the teachers of my school days laid the first stones with patient and cheerful hands, and the professors of IISER Kolkata, taking up the work where the schoolmasters gently set it down, carried the building higher, into the very regions of inquiry and of thought, where the young mind learns not merely to answer, but to ask.

I thank you, my school teachers, first and without reserve, for the constancy of your presence; for the mornings you arrived before us and the evenings you remained behind, as though the schoolroom were a small lighthouse upon a darkening coast, and you its patient keeper, trimming the wick that we might find our way; and in that quiet and unremarked devotion I found the very model of what it is to be dependable in an age that has half forgotten the word.

And I thank you, my professors at IISER Kolkata, for receiving that same pupil when he came to you older, and for treating him not as a vessel to be filled but as a colleague in the making; for the seminars wherein you suffered our questions gladly, and the laboratories wherein you let us fail and fail again until the failure itself became instruction, and the open doors and unhurried conversations by which you showed that the pursuit of science is not a race but a devotion, pursued best in company.

I thank you all for your trust, which you extended to me more generously than my timid years deserved; you set upon my shoulders tasks a little heavier than I believed I could carry, whether those tasks were a school project trembling in the hand of a boy, or a research problem set humming in the mind of a young scholar, and then you waited, with that calm assurance which the wise possess and the anxious so sorely lack, and in the waiting you taught me that confidence is not a gift bestowed but a harvest earned, and that the field, however stony, will answer at last to patient ploughing.

Nor were your lessons confined to the narrow province of studies; you opened instead the wider province of life itself, and bade me speak of doubt as freely as of algebra, of sorrow as plainly as of science; the schoolroom became a harbour for a boy drifting upon the uncertain waters of growing up, and the corridors and offices of IISER Kolkata became a harbour in their turn for the young man who still, on occasion, drifts; and in both harbours I was heard, and not judged, and rose again with lighter ballast for having spoken.

And I thank you, most of all perhaps, for the inspiration you dealt so freely and so often that it seemed no treasure at all until I found myself abroad in the world, rich in resolve, and traced the riches back to you; for every time the spirit faltered, whether before a school examination or a research presentation, some remembered sentence of yours rose up like a small lantern in a long gallery, and I went on, and went on gladly.

Now observe, I pray you, this small wonder: that the very letter you are reading, unfolding itself in three dimensions before your eyes, wreathed in flowers of light and set to music, was written in code by the hand of your own pupil, and by the hand of your own student; and that the hand was taught, and the mind was formed, and the courage was kindled, first within the schoolroom walls and afterwards within the halls and laboratories of IISER Kolkata; so that this website, with its turning pages and its singing flowers, is not merely a greeting but a certificate of your own handiwork, a testimonial written in the very skills you gave me, saying: you taught a child to write, to think, to question, to build; and behold what the child, and the undergraduate, have built for you.

If one pupil, thus equipped by school and institute alike, can send his gratitude across the internet to find you wherever you dwell, then consider how far your teaching must travel, borne invisibly in a thousand minds you will never meet, a blessing going forth perpetually, as ripples go forth from a stone cast kindly into still water.

With love and endless gratitude,

Shuvam
